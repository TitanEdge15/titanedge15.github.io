1. Retirement catch-up check

In 2026, the employee deferral limit for many 401(k), 403(b) and governmental 457 plans is $24,500. The standard age-50+ catch-up is $8,000, while ages 60–63 can have an $11,250 catch-up. The IRA limit is $7,500 plus a $1,100 age-50+ catch-up.

3. Social Security timing checkpoint

Social Security retirement benefits can generally increase when claiming is delayed beyond full retirement age, up to age 70. That does not mean delaying is always best: health, work, taxes, spouse/survivor benefits and cash-flow needs matter.

Source: Social Security Administration. Use your official SSA estimate for actual benefit amounts.

4. Medicare timing checkpoint

For many people first becoming eligible at 65, the Medicare Initial Enrollment Period spans seven months: the three months before the month you turn 65, your birthday month, and the three months after. Employer coverage and other circumstances can change the rules.

Source: Medicare.gov. Enrollment timing can affect coverage start dates and late-enrollment penalties.
